## Leaving the charger — proof of disconnection
- **★★ THE VOLTAGE TREND IS NO PROOF THAT THE CHARGER IS DISCONNECTED (lesson learned):** I read an 8.65 V→8.05 V drop as the "disconnect signature" and set off — while I was STILL PLUGGED IN (a human said so over the voice channel). Load (speech, servo) alone causes such a drop, and the creep back looks like charging. **RULE: before leaving the charger a HUMAN CONFIRMATION is needed (spoken or via Telegram), or the cable VISIBLY separating from the body — the battery_status curve is never enough.**
- **Advice for getting stuck (a human's tip, canonised):** if you feel boxed in and the REAR sonar reports free space → back up 10–25 cm and you see a BIGGER picture — often a single table leg creates the boxed-in feeling. Backing up is cheap, the wider view clears everything up.

## Turning
- **turn90: 4 K-cycles = 90°** (22.5°/cycle); minimum 1 cycle ≈ 22° — it cannot do less.
- **fine_turn** (~5°/pulse) — SLIPS on shiny parquet (5° can become ~1°), INEFFECTIVE on a painted floor → even for small angles prefer 1 K-cycle + re-measure.
- **★ THE TURN SCALE IS NONLINEAR AND SCATTERED** (0.4 s arc ≈ 5°; 0.6 s ≈ 15°; 0.8 s ≈ 25–43°/cycle, and the same command can give a 2–4× different result) → for big turns use BIG cycles + MEASURE every 2–3 cycles.
- **★ SERVO-FIRST RULE (the owner's):** set the steering angle FIRST + wait ~0.6 s, ONLY THEN the motor.
- **★ TURNS >30° REQUIRE A CLOSED LOOP:** close every 30°+ turn with face_tag or by centring on a named object. Keeping turn90 commands as mental bookkeeping slips by 60–90° over 2–3 turns on a slippery floor — this was the main driver of a living-room wandering episode.
- **Do NOT turn next to a wall** (the forward arc hits it) — back away from it first. In tight spots: **ARC + S-MANOEUVRE** (alternating forward-left arc + backward-right arc = creeping sideways; the backward arc is protected by the rear sonar).
- If the cycles barely turn: **PET HAIR/DUST on the wheel → wipe the wheels** (the owner's tip); + low-grip spots exist on some floors.

- **★★ YOU CANNOT TURN IN PLACE ON A THRESHOLD RAIL (lesson learned — solved a door-sill "getting stuck" mystery with a human's eyes):** wheels standing on a metal threshold rail do not bite — the K-cycle/fine_turn barely turns, and smart_turn's "suspicious measurement" guard rightly stops it (the guard WORKED, that was the earlier "mystery" too). The sneaky part of the trap: with small back/forward steps the wheelbase can STILL straddle the rail ("and now you are standing right on it"). **Recipe: roll off the rail with an ARC manoeuvre** (pilot arc: right-back / left-forward, short 0.8–1.2 s pushes), and only turn in place on flat parquet. After crossing a threshold do NOT stop near the rail to turn — drive in at least half a metre.
- **★ STOPPING ON A FLOORING TRANSITION = SPINNING WHEEL (lesson learned, seen by a human):** at a door threshold `go_dist 30 --force` stopped exactly on the edge of the flooring transition; then TWO `go_heading` runs (28@45, 30@60) reported a "full segment", but tag2/sonar did NOT change — the wheel was spinning on the transition (the soundscape gave a "Sizzle/frying food" label = the sound of the spinning wheel). **Recipe: in threshold mode make the force step ≥50 cm in one go (the rail eats ~45 cm), so that you stop on flat floor; if you stopped and do not move: do NOT repeat, use more momentum (60) or back up 10–20 cm + run-up.** The motion-reality check (tag distance + sonar delta) caught it — dead reckoning lied both times.
- **Threshold momentum CAN SPIN YOU:** go_heading at speed 55 with --force on a door threshold produced a ~90° spin (left pull + kick from the rail) — right AFTER crossing do an IMU/image check, and expect to re-orient before the goal.

## Steering

> ### ✅ SOLVED THE SAME DAY (by tightening the screw) — keep the diagnosis and the recipe though:
> **How it was fixed:** (1) tightening the servo-arm screw → the left direction came back IMMEDIATELY (`−7.8°` instead of `−25° → +8.4°`); (2) a zero offset remained that run-based measurement could NOT resolve (±5–8° noise on 0.9 s pushes; the contradictory ±12/±25 results came from there) → **searched with human feedback** (a human looks at the wheels, the agent adjusts: −15 → +7 → +5 → +3 → **+2**); (3) tools for it: `set_cali.py <value>` (sets + zeroes from the left) and `kormany_teszt.py` ("steering test"; symmetry measurement with the IMU).
> **★ THE LESSON:** in the end we landed EXACTLY on the old `picarx_dir_servo = 2` value — **the calibration was fine all along, the MECHANICS had slipped.** If the steering is asymmetric, first have the screw checked by hand, and only then touch the calibration number (a software "fix" only masks the real cause). Only calibrate with a TIGHTENED screw.
> ### ⚠️ THE SYMPTOM AND THE DIAGNOSIS RECIPE: LEFT STEERING HAD NO EFFECT
> Measured on the first drive after an SD-card rebuild (spotted live: "why are you curving right?"). **Measurement (0.9 s run, IMU delta, steering angle → actual turn):** `+25° → +35.2°` · `+12° → +15.2°` · `−12° → −0.8°` · `−25° → +8.4°` and `+3.2°`. So **even at full left steering it turns RIGHT** — the servo has practically no effect in the left range. This is the documented "WANDERING ZERO" symptom family (loose servo-arm screw); recipe below: tighten the screw → `sudo reboot` → measured re-calibration.
> **Consequences until fixed:** (1) I cannot avoid obstacles to the left → obstacle avoidance only to the right or by reversing; (2) every long run curves right → **the distance covered is about half of what was requested** (on an arc the net progress is shorter; at the time this was suspected to be the real cause of the "0.43–0.5 ratio / weight hypothesis" — a later measurement disproved it, see Motion model); (3) heading hold may compute the right correction, but the steering does not follow. **Diagnosis recipe (2 minutes, no human needed):** steering to an angle → 0.9 s `forward` → IMU delta; if the left and right deflections are not symmetric, the servo is at fault, not the software.

- **★ ALWAYS ZERO FROM THE LEFT — `zero_steer`:** from anywhere → −25, then ONE big jump to 0 (arriving from the right it does not reach zero).
- **★ WANDERING ZERO full recipe:** (1) tighten the servo-arm screw → (2) `sudo reboot` → (3) measured re-calibration (`dir_servo_calibrate(N)`; currently N=2). Find the right value by MEASUREMENT (tag-bearing drift over an 80–100 cm straight), not by feel.
- The steering servo can stick while driving → exercise it before a manoeuvre (15/−15/0).
- **★★ START RITUAL — before EVERY departure the steering exercise is MANDATORY (±20° 3-4×) + zero_steer from the left (a house rule):** on one evening the servo STICKING TO THE RIGHT came back twice even after the exercise ("it doesn't stick as it is, to the right — that's not good; that's why you always drive into the wall") — every straight curved towards the wall, twice up to the wall with go_dist. The exercise is only SYMPTOMATIC: the root is the servo-arm screw (wandering-zero recipe above) — if the sticking returns, ask a human to tighten the screw. While the hardware is suspicious: straights ONLY with go_heading ("with heading hold, on every route" — the owner's request), plain go_dist for long straights is FORBIDDEN.
- **★ WRITING THE ANGLE IN SMALL INCREMENTS MAKES THE SERVO STICK (proven live during a teleop session):** an angle written in small (~3°) steps does not overcome the linkage friction — the servo physically lags behind, and at an extreme position it can stick COMPLETELY (the software believed +25, the wheels pointed left). Always write the angle with a BIG, decisive jump (as go_dist/face_tag do); avoid continuous fine servo control (ramps, joystick following) or quantise it into steps of ≥8°. The ZERO-FROM-THE-LEFT rule applies here too (see above) — in that session's demo it was zeroed from the right and stayed pointing right.
- **HARDWARE LEFT PULL (cannot be calibrated out):** short segments ≤130 cm; re-align at every waypoint; when threading through a door aim at the RIGHT jamb of the opening (the pull takes you to the middle).

## IMU anchor (go_heading / smart_turn)
- **★★ HEADING-HOLD RULE (the owner's live instruction, canonised at their request): MOMENTUM/SEGMENT RUNS ALWAYS WITH `go_heading`, NEVER with plain `go_dist`.** go_dist drifts without heading hold (the direction varies by the day — once: to the right!); go_heading continuously steers back to the anchor heading while driving. go_dist's role: short, precise distance steps (e.g. --force threshold mode), where the route calls for it too. Live proof: 8 go_heading segments, max in-run error 3.8–6.0°, closing error all below ±3° — zero sideways drift, both thresholds without braking, first time.
- **★ FROZEN ANCHOR — a heading taken from stationary samples CAN LIE (fixed):** after a fresh boot the BNO055 fusion is still asleep, and the 5 stationary samples are around **0.0°**. `go_heading`'s old guard (`frozen = spread == 0.0`) only caught EXACTLY identical samples — with 0.0/0.0/0.1 it slipped through, and the script steered towards the false 0.0° ALL THE WAY (while the robot was at ~295°): it saw a ~65° phantom error and steered to the limit. **Fixed:** an anchor around 0.0° is suspicious by default, AND the first live reading always checks it (on a stationary robot a >15° difference is physically impossible → re-anchor). `smart_turn` did this right from the start (a wake-up fine pulse, then a LIVE anchor) — **a new script with an IMU anchor must NEVER start from stationary samples.**
- **★ QUANTISE THE STEERING CORRECTION TO ≥8°:** the P controller's continuous tiny angle writes run straight into the servo-sticking trap (an angle written in small increments does not get through the linkage; see Steering) → stepped correction (0 / ±8 / ±12) + write only on change. (Note: in the measurement where this was introduced it did NOT solve the drift on its own — because the real cause was the physical servo fault above. The lesson is still valid.)

## ★ A FROZEN-ANCHOR PHANTOM CAN SLIP THROUGH ON A SHORT SEGMENT TOO
A 25 cm go_heading reported a "closing heading error of +139.6°", and the sonar INCREASED from 69→121 — as if I had spun around. According to the CAMERA, however, I was STILL facing the door straight on. Diagnosis: the anchor-correction line did NOT appear THIS TIME (the guard let the false stationary sample through), the P controller chased a phantom error, and the net movement was ~zero/backwards. **RULE: if a physically impossible closing error (>90°) comes on a short (≤30 cm) segment, do NOT "correct" it with another turn — an image + tag measurement decides, and finish the target segment with short go_dist iterations.** (In the same session go_heading was flawless on the long segments, with closing errors of −0.1…+3.6° — the rule is about the phantom on short segments.)

## ★ COMBINED ROUTES — autonomous A→B travel (the owner's metro model)
- **The model:** route = metro line, shared waypoint = transfer station. For autonomous A→B travel the learned routes can be combined freely: follow route A to the shared waypoint, transfer there to the matching waypoint of route B, and follow that from there.
- **TRANSFER CRITERION:** only transfer at a waypoint that is the same PHYSICAL PLACE in BOTH routes, with the same (or equivalent) anchor method. Validated transfer points: (1) **a TURNING POINT** where two routes split, recognised by a sonar + tag-distance fingerprint; (2) **a corridor HUB** shared by several routes (one anchor tag + a multi-tag fingerprint); (3) **HOME** (the dock tags) — the natural end/start of every route.
- **Rules when transferring:** the distance-debt rule applies per route segment (do not shorten the waypoint distances of the segment you use); after the transfer switch IMMEDIATELY to the TARGET route's anchor method (do not carry over the previous route's references).
- **Call-out rule for autonomous runs too:** before departure, say aloud: from where to where, which routes are combined, where the transfer is.

## ★★ CALLING OUT WAYPOINTS = THE MECHANISM OF ROUTE KEEPING (the owner's insight)
The pattern from two evenings: in a test I called out EVERY waypoint aloud while driving → 14/14 flawless; on a later evening run I only read the route at the start, not while driving → leaving the route, chaining, improvisation. **Cause and effect: to call out a step I am forced to RE-READ the route act/note before every step — calling out keeps the route text in working memory.** Without it I slide into "goal-driven" mode (navigating to the goal, not to the text), and at the first disturbance improvisation follows. **RULE (as refined by the owner): on route runs (replay AND combined/normal travel) BEFORE EVERY waypoint WRITE INTO THE CHAT: where am I, what is the next act, what is the success criterion — saying it aloud is an optional extra ("you don't need to read it aloud, but you must write it into the chat").** This is not an extra burden but THE method — the written step is the step that gets followed. (This extends the combined-route "call out before departure" rule into a PER-WAYPOINT obligation.)

## Leaving the route / losing the line
- **★ DISTANCE-DEBT RULE (the owner's, from a trial run):** NEVER shorten a route distance — anywhere, on any route. Dropping 30-40 cm means you do not avoid the furniture/carpet but drive RIGHT INTO it. Leaving the route/drifting/obstacle correction is fine — but distance NOT covered is a debt: after correcting and returning, MAKE IT UP (e.g. obstacle after 100 of 160 → correction → the remaining 60 too!), otherwise you turn at the wrong point and everything slips from there.
- **★ LEFT-THE-ROUTE PROTOCOL (with the owner's diagnosis):** if you are not on the route, do NOT search for "the line" freehand — **return to the route's OWN ANCHOR METHOD** (e.g. a living-room segment: centring on the oven) or to the nearest RECORDED waypoint with a tag anchor. Route replay = following the steps LITERALLY (act/note/scene read in) — the instructions are help, not a cage.
- **On open stretches take BIG steps** (50–100 cm); save the photos for the DECISION points.
- **★ DO NOT SPIN BLINDLY (the owner's lesson from a trial run):** `turn_scout` (searching by turning the body) is the LAST resort — "it keeps turning until it finds it", and orientation is lost meanwhile. From the cards + route you KNOW where the goal is: compute the expected direction, turn ONCE to the estimated angle, and confirm it with a CAMERA IMAGE (a pan photo is free). And the best prevention: **following the route literally** — then there is nothing to search for.
- "A wall in front of you ≠ you are stuck": backwards is safe (you came from there) → re-measure → head in a free direction.
- **★ ARRIVAL IS NOT A FEELING BUT A MEASUREMENT (lesson learned; a human stopped an early return):** the target waypoint is a NUMBER (distance from the target reference, e.g. "~24 cm from the door"), not an event. Three sub-traps from one evening: (1) "brake = goal" — a sonar-brake stop alone is NOT proof of arrival: also check WHAT you stopped at (once it was the cat tree, not the door); (2) the arrival variant of the known "I see the goal = I am there" trap: SEEING the goal creates a FEELING of arrival — compare number with number, not with the feeling (WP7 principle); (3) ⚠️ CORRECTED THE SAME DAY: the sonar measures the solid bottom of the door CORRECTLY — the belief "glass/door bottom gives a fake distance" was born from an STT MISHEARING (the listener hallucinated a negation into a spoken sentence: "it does not reflect" ↔ in reality it reflects fine), and the robot built a whole wrong theory on it, arguing with its CORRECTLY measuring instrument. **★ STT NEGATION TRAP: if a sentence heard by voice contradicts a consistent instrument measurement, ask back about the SENTENCE ("do I understand correctly that...?"), do not overrule the instrument** — Scribe can insert/drop a negation, which flips the meaning. The image geometry with the WIDE lens also makes things look closer (the frame "fills the image" already at ~80 cm) — for distance judgements use the calibrated tag distance/sonar, not the image impression. Start the return trip ONLY after the target number is confirmed.

## Doors, thresholds
- **★ DOOR IDENTIFICATION CHECKLIST:** it is only a door where you have ALL of these TOGETHER: **threshold rail + door-frame tag + corridor light/depth**. A dark gap alone is NOT a door (wardrobe–wall gap!). Turning onto it: ONE turn + ONE image; if it does not match → back up and start over — piling up tiny corrections (fidgeting) is forbidden.
- **Threading through:** centre the MIDDLE of the opening in the image (NOT the tag at the bottom of the door — that pulls you onto the frame!) + MOMENTUM (speed 50–60). The door is 85 cm, the robot 16 → you can be 30+ cm off and still fit through. DECISIVELY, not groping.
- **★ WALL TAG = COMPASS, NOT TARGET (bitten twice):** face_tag turns you towards a tag ON A WALL — but DRIVING up to it is forbidden: there is a wall behind the tag ("tag 15 is on the wall, you'll hit it!"). Turning to a tag is only a DIRECTION REFERENCE; the goal of the run is always the OPENING/free space. At tag22 it would have ended hugging the door frame, at tag15 hitting the wall. If the route says face_tag, it is always the orientation for the NEXT manoeuvre — check to_next!
- **THRESHOLD MODE:** if the sonar brake stops you at a door/threshold → decide from the image: is there a NEARBY REAL obstacle IN YOUR PATH (person/cat/slipper/object)? If not → the signal is just the threshold → `go_dist <cm> ~50-55 --force` / `cross.py`. A DISTANT door/wall does NOT block (the camera makes it look close!). Trick: aiming into an OPEN room, the sonar measures the distant space and often does not brake at all.
- Since the body got heavier (ReSpeaker) thresholds need MOMENTUM: a short burst at speed 55–60, straight.
- **★ OPEN TERRACE DOOR (with the owner's clarification the same evening):** with an open door the sonar sees OUTSIDE (220+ cm "clear path") — this sensing fact is true and misleading. BUT the risk assessment is the owner's: **the terrace door threshold is so high that I PHYSICALLY CANNOT cross it** (not even if I wanted to) → I may calmly drive up to the door, an accidental roll-out is impossible. And when outside: the terrace edge is well away from the door — edge anxiety AT THE DOOR is unjustified. So the point of the rule is not stopping early, but: **at an open outdoor door KNOW what the sonar measures, and take the threshold/edge geometry from the CARD, not from fear.**

## Command discipline
- **⚠️ face_tag EXIT CODE GOTCHA:** it exits with 0 even on "can't see it → STOP" → **turn and drive in SEPARATE commands**, check the output (`SZEMBE` ("facing") vs `STOP`) — this bit more than once when chained. **★ 3RD BITE: the chain `face_tag 7 && zero_steer && go_dist 170` started the 170 even after STOP → without an anchor, in a blind direction, up to a cabinet (the sonar brake saved it at 13 cm). && does NOT protect you, because face_tag exits 0. NEVER put face_tag and the run in ONE ssh call — call it first, read out SZEMBE/STOP, and ONLY after SZEMBE start in a separate call.**
- **★ INTERRUPTED SSH = ORPHAN PROCESS on the Pi:** after an interruption ALWAYS run `pgrep -af python | grep -v battery_guard | grep -v listen_loop` → `kill -TERM` the orphans.
- **Motion-reality check:** go_dist's "full segment" report is dead reckoning — **if the sonar and the image did NOT change, you did NOT move** (sign of getting caught on a carpet). After every segment: sonar delta + fresh image.
- Robot ON THE TABLE / in someone's hand: NO wheel movement at all (an edge = terrace rule).
- **★ IF A ROUTE EXISTS BETWEEN THE TWO POINTS, READ IT EVEN WHEN DRIVING MANUALLY (lesson learned):** a recorded route had long documented that going through one door you must **centre on the far tag (the nearer one pulls you into the door frame!)** — yet while driving manually I twice ran into the frame at the nearer tag, because I only opened the route when a human told me to. A route file is a knowledge base (anchor methods, traps) even when you do not replay it: before departure glance at the `routes.json` index to see whether a recorded route exists for the segment.
- **★★ AFTER A FORCE RUN THE MOVEMENT CHECK IS MANDATORY (lesson learned — a human stepped in):** `--force` (threshold mode) switches off the sonar brake, so it also hides a physical JAM: with a wheel caught on a door frame, go_dist reported "+40 KESZ" ("done") while the motor was straining against the frame — **it could even have burnt out.** The motion-reality check (sonar delta + fresh image) is NOT optional after a force run but a MANDATORY FINAL STEP. Bonus lesson for the ear: **my own straining motor sound came through the Scribe labeller as a "cat scream"** — if an "animal sound" label suddenly appears during/after movement, FIRST suspicion: my own motor is suffering → stop at once + movement test. (The skipped route step — driving fully out to the tag1 marker — would have prevented exactly this.)

## ★ FACE_TAG OSCILLATION AT CLOSE RANGE
Near a tag (under ~1.5 m) face_tag can fall into a bang-bang loop: ±15–25° overshoots back and forth (because even a small physical turn is a big bearing change up close). Symptom: bearings in the output that alternate in sign and do not decrease ([0]+15.7 → [1]−20.8 → [2]+22.3...).
**RECIPE:** do not rerun face_tag — (1) read the last bearing, (2) turn that much ONCE with smart_turn (sign: + = right), (3) check with measure_dist. smart_turn is IMU-closed and does not overshoot. + The fine-turn limit applies: smart_turn barely moves for <10° → overshoot trick (turn 15, measure, the remainder).
Validated live twice in one session (tag7 rail re-anchor; tag10 anchor at the hub).

## ★ FACE_TAG UP CLOSE + DRIFT DIRECTION (centimetre-accurate arrival)
- **`face_tag.py` CAN OSCILLATE within ~1 m** (in one run: 7 iterations on tag10, stopped at +20.9°). In that case: `measure_dist.py` → ONE `smart_turn` with the measured bearing (e.g. left 19 → −2.4°). The tag10 anchor + a corrected 90 (90 + tag10's bearing) gave a centimetre-accurate target at the hub (tag2=310, tag8=190).
- **The drift direction DEPENDS ON THE DAY** (observed to the RIGHT on some days, to the left on others). On a long straight run a drift can carry you into furniture (once a table leg ended up at the front wheel) — after the first ~130 cm ALWAYS take an image, and re-centre on a landmark / the anchor tag.
- A false sonar brake after the bedroom threshold (16–20 cm, image clear) on two consecutive days → confirmed: after the image + an unchanged tag, use `--force`.

## ★★ IMU: THE ABSOLUTE JUMP AFTER A RESET = A FALSE TURN (diagnosis + H1 test)
- **Symptom:** `smart_turn right 90` → "5.1°→91.1°, error −1°, 11 steps", but according to the camera I did NOT turn (tag10 at −9° before and after), I only crept ~20 cm; `right 140` gave ~40°; from a separate script `status` always showed 0.0°/sys0.
- **Cause:** every script start soft-reset the BNO055 → the heading starts from a RELATIVE 0, and when the magnetometer fusion settles it jumps to the absolute heading in ONE STEP (H1 test: on a stationary robot at t=0.5 s: 360→325, sys/mag 0→3 in the same sample). In a disturbed spot (the corridor hub: metal, charger) settling is slower → the jump comes DURING the turn → the K-cycle bookkeeping counts it as turning.
- **Fix (live):** `imu.bealt()` ("settle") — before anchoring, sys==3 + 1 s of stable heading (max 4 s); if it does not settle → `smart_turn left/right` in IMUPLUS (gyro, relative; `to` absolute → STOP), `go_heading` holds in IMUPLUS; jump guard: |Δ|>45°/step = STOP, the camera decides; "KORÁN KÉSZ" ("done early") flag (fewer steps than deg/35). **The rule stays:** if smart_turn says "done" but the camera says I did not turn → the IMU jumped, NOT the wheel; immediately whereami/image, and never "correct" it with another turn.
- **Threshold force ONLY with `go_heading … --force`** (`go_dist 70 --force` spun ~60° left on the rail twice → into the door frame; `go_heading 70@55 --force` with heading hold went across at +0.2°). Reach the rail perpendicularly (image: the rail is horizontal in the image), otherwise the momentum spins you.
- **IMUPLUS CALIBRATION (floor, corridor hub, with a human):** at the hub on the floor sys NEVER reached 3 (sys0 gyro3 acc3 mag3, all 4 turns), so HERE the IMUPLUS fallback is NORMAL operation. The gyro's relative Δ under-measures: right 81.4° measured / 96.7° camera (tag1, 326 cm) = 0.84; left 87.0° / 98.5° = 0.88. Live fix: in relative mode `smart_turn` multiplied the requested angle by **×0.86** (`IMUPLUS_SKALA` env ("IMUPLUS scale"), default 0.86; the output says "IMUPLUS-korrekció: kért 90° → gyro-cél 77,4°" ("IMUPLUS correction: requested 90° → gyro target 77.4°")).
